{% comment %} The masthead, and the whole of the shared identity on a documentation site. It repeats the reidmorrison.com wordmark construction exactly: the shield to the left and OUTSIDE the 2px rule, the project name in Spectral sitting on that rule, the tagline in letterspaced mono beneath. Someone who reads a gem's docs and later lands on reidmorrison.com should recognise one hand at work. That recognition is the entire commercial value here, and it is why there is no nav to /services, no entity block and no pricing anywhere on these sites. See CLAUDE.md, "What is shared and what is not". The mark comes from _includes/logo.svg. This theme ships the plain RM shield as the default; a site with its own drops one at docs/_includes/logo.svg and it wins, because Jekyll searches the site's _includes before the theme's. That file must be inline SVG rather than an : its fills read the --logo-ink and --logo-accent custom properties, and an externally referenced SVG cannot see the page's CSS. A project whose identity is an ILLUSTRATION rather than a monogram sets `project.mark_image` instead, and gets an . Rocket Job's rocket is the case this exists for: a detailed, full-colour drawing does not reduce to two flat fills, so the custom-property trick has nothing to work with. The tradeoff is that such a mark cannot respond to the theme, so it must be legible on both grounds on its own. Check it against --surface in both before shipping one. Deliberately NOT sticky. On a page with forty sections, vertical room is worth more than a persistent header, and the sidebar is what needs to stay in view. The old layouts had a sticky header and a matching scroll-padding script; both are gone. {% endcomment %}
